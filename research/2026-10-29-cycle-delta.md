### Pillar 5: Passwordless / split-key

- **Ofcom endorses facial age estimation and three other methods as "highly effective" for the UK under-16 social media ban — published Oct 29.** Ofcom's rapid assessment, delivered to Parliament at the Secretary of State's request, formally names four methods capable of reliably determining 16+ status: photo ID matching, digital ID services, banking-based checks, and facial age estimation. Key nuance: Ofcom says facial estimation has "a margin of error of a few years at 16" and recommends a "challenge age" approach — services should use estimation to trigger a secondary ID check rather than as a standalone gate. Ofcom also notes that 16–17 year-olds who lack photo ID "would likely need to take steps to obtain one." The government plans to publish its first regulations before end-2026, with the under-16 social media restrictions coming into force in 2027. This is the first Ofcom positive finding on facial estimation for the 16 threshold; January 2025 guidance covered only the 18 threshold and listed seven methods.

### Pillar 6: LATAM

- **BCB bank-VASP counterparty ban takes effect tomorrow (Oct 30); today is the final VASP filing deadline.** Under BCB Resolution 519/520, existing SPSAVs (virtual asset service providers) must have filed their authorization requests by end of October 29 to continue operating while the BCB processes their applications. From October 30, all BCB-supervised financial and payment institutions are prohibited from conducting or facilitating virtual-asset operations with counterparties that lack BCB authorization or a pending application. VASPs that missed today's deadline must cease operations within 30 days. As of September 2026, no SPSAV authorization had been publicly announced by the BCB, meaning the ban will affect a large portion of the Brazilian VASP market from tomorrow. Resolution 589/2026 may have moved the effective bank-ban date to November 6 — see Oct 28 delta for context; verify against official BCB text.
