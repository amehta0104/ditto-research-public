## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **A new Android malware pairing (WindRelay + SpyNote) defeats the two assumptions almost every bank's authentication stack rests on: that a dynamic contactless cryptogram can't be replayed, and that a session inside the bank's own app on the customer's own device is trustworthy. The attacker relays the victim's live NFC card exchange to a real payment terminal elsewhere while the victim holds the card — and, in the documented case, originates a loan in the victim's name from inside their banking app. Attack chain to cash-out: ~13 minutes.** Account: / company. Angle: this is not a "mobile malware is rising" post — that framing is dead per `fraud-deepfake.md`. The POV is architectural: **the industry has spent a decade hardening the credential and almost nothing hardening the runtime that holds it.** PSD2 SCA treats the mobile device as the possession factor; when the device is remotely controlled, SCA completes *correctly* and the fraud is indistinguishable from a legitimate customer action. Note precisely what dynamic NFC data did and did not buy: the cryptogram is still single-use and transaction-bound — the attackers didn't break it, they **moved it fast enough**, which means freshness guarantees fail against a live relay. The structural answers are the ones Ditto sells: real-time device-integrity signalling (accessibility-service abuse, RAT presence, hooking, screen capture) as a *precondition* of authentication rather than a post-hoc risk score, and split-key binding so no single compromised system — including the customer's own phone — can authorise alone. Strongest pairing available: the MILCOM 2026 5G prototype below independently arrives at the same conclusion from the network side — attest the device *before* you authenticate the subscriber — for a measured 373ms. POV hook: "A fraud crew called a bank customer, talked them into installing an app, and 13 minutes later had taken out a loan in their name and was tapping their card at a shop in another city — while the card sat in the customer's pocket. Nothing cryptographic broke. The one-time code was still one-time; it was just relayed live. The customer's device was still the possession factor; it was just being driven by someone else. Every control in that chain worked exactly as designed and the money left anyway. That is what it looks like when you verify the credential and never verify the machine holding it." Threat-intel datapoint — **do not name the affected banks** (Group-IB does not name them; the report identifies target countries only), and do not cite the $355K figure, which belongs to a different campaign.

### Pillar 2: Identity orchestration

- **Researchers propose InterSAGE, a four-layer identity and trust architecture for AI agents — binding developer, code package, operator and deployment context into a single verifiable credential (reported 2026-08-14)** — InterSAGE proposes giving AI agents persistent identities, verifiable capability records, bounded authorisation and cryptographic accountability when they interact across organisational boundaries. Components: an **Agent Identity Card** binding developer + code package + operator + deployment context into one verifiable credential; a **Global Agent Registry** that signs the credential and handles key provisioning, rotation and revocation; **DID-linked credentials** describing available skills and tools; **narrowing authorisation** as authority is delegated down a chain; and **kernel-mediated records** tying each action back to the agent that performed it. Explicitly positioned to *complement* rather than replace agent communication protocols (MCP, A2A, ANP, AG-UI) — its contribution is a common trust layer carried across technical environments. Status caveat: the authors call it a **positioning paper, not a production-ready specification**; proofs, benchmarks and implementations are future work. For: this is the clearest published articulation yet of the architecture `identity-orchestration.md` Sub-theme E has been circling — "authenticate user → delegate scope to agent → bind agent intent to verifiable credentials → audit machine actions." Two details are strategically useful. First, **key rotation and revocation are named as first-class registry functions** — which is the same reissuance/revocation problem `eudi-readiness.md` flags as "where most vendors will break first," now restated for machine identities. Second, **authorisation narrows as it is delegated**, which is the orchestration-layer answer to agentic scope creep and maps onto continuous-trust rather than one-shot verification. Feeds the September NHI/agentic sweep with an architecture proposal rather than another M&A datapoint. Not in prior deltas.

### Pillar 8: Mobile trust & app security

- **PRIMARY — Group-IB documents WindRelay: an NFC-relay malware paired with SpyNote RAT that replays a victim's live contactless card transaction to an attacker's terminal in real time, and originates a loan from inside the victim's own banking app. Attack chain to cash-out ~13 minutes (Group-IB 2026-08-12; in-window secondary pickup 2026-08-14 — first-capture)** — The chain has three steps. (1) **Social engineering**: a caller impersonating a bank employee tells the customer there is a problem with their card and keeps them on the line. (2) **RAT install**: the victim sideloads a personalised fake banking app carrying **SpyNote**, which abuses Android **Accessibility Services** for remote control — notably **without triggering screen-share detection**. (3) **Silent second-stage + relay**: using SpyNote's remote access the operator installs **WindRelay** with no further victim interaction, then instructs the victim to tap their card. WindRelay turns the phone into a fraudulent contactless reader and streams the live NFC exchange — **including the card's transaction-specific authentication data** — to the attacker's device, which presents it at a genuine payment terminal. In the documented case the operator also used the victim's banking app to **take out a loan in the victim's name**, giving a dual digital-and-physical cash-out. WindRelay additionally reads contacts for onward targeting and requests an unusual `DUMP` permission for device inspection. Scope: **23 samples uploaded to VirusTotal between November 2025 and July 2026**; **4 command-and-control addresses**; primary targets **Czechia, Slovakia and Slovenia**, with samples localised to mimic named local financial institutions and carrying victim-specific labels. Group-IB publishes **no aggregate victim count or loss figure for WindRelay** (see integrity notes — the $355K figure in the post belongs to a separate earlier campaign). Why this matters for Ditto, precisely: dynamic contactless cryptograms are the control that is supposed to make card data replay-proof, and **the attackers did not break it — they outran it**, which means freshness guarantees provide no protection against a live relay from a compromised device. Simultaneously, PSD2 SCA's treatment of the mobile device as the possession factor fails **silently**: with the device under remote control, authentication completes correctly and the fraudulent action is indistinguishable from a genuine one at every layer above the runtime. This is `mobile-trust.md` Sub-themes A (accessibility-service abuse / man-in-the-app), C (malware install → ATO → cash-out lifecycle) and D (the SCA mobile assumption) in a single documented campaign, and it is the strongest available case for device-integrity signalling as a **precondition** of authentication rather than a downstream risk score. Do not name affected banks — Group-IB identifies countries only. Not in prior deltas (Group-IB's 12 Aug publication fell inside the 08-14 delta's window and was missed).
