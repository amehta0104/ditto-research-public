### Pillar 6: Identity verification (IDV)
- **Ofcom's first Age Assurance Report reframes the IDV bar for age: "highly effective" age assurance is now a tested regulatory standard, and behavioural age-inference does not meet it (July 16, 2026)** — See full writeup under Pillar 11. The IDV-specific significance: Ofcom is drawing a hard line between *highly effective* age-assurance methods (its published guidance list — e.g. photo-ID matching, facial age *estimation* with defined thresholds, credit-reference/open-banking checks, digital-ID wallets) and *age-inference* (estimating age from on-platform behaviour), ruling the latter not capable of being highly effective. For an IDV vendor this is a market-shaping signal: platforms currently leaning on behavioural inference to avoid friction now face a compliance-driven push toward verifiable, method-certified age checks — and the winning approach must clear both the effectiveness bar *and* the privacy bar (see EU ZKP convergence, P11).

### Pillar 11: Age assurance & privacy attributes
- **Ofcom publishes its first statutory Age Assurance Report and opens a formal TikTok investigation the same day — behavioural age-inference declared not "highly effective," platforms told to switch now (July 16, 2026)** — Ofcom released its first statutory report under the Online Safety Act on how services have used age assurance in the first year of the child-safety duties (in force since 25 Jul 2025). Headline data: the proportion of children who, when asked to prove their age, encountered *highly effective* age checks rose from **25% to 43% between July 2025 and January 2026**, with age checks now deployed at "unprecedented scale" across pornography, social media, dating and gaming. Enforcement: Ofcom has opened **23 investigations into providers of 88 adult services**; all of the UK's top-10 porn sites and **64 of the top-100** now run age checks (10 have geo-blocked UK users), though "too many porn sites still don't have age checks in place." Critically for identity vendors, Ofcom drew a sharp methodological line: services "which use age *inference* models to comply with their child protection duties should switch to other methods listed in our guidance as highly effective **without delay**," and it stated age inference is "not included in our industry guidance as a method that is capable of being highly effective for this purpose." On the same day, Ofcom opened a **formal investigation into TikTok** under s.12 OSA — group director Kate Davies said Ofcom has "very serious questions about whether age inference can be highly effective," with evidence TikTok's inference methods "may be failing to correctly detect significant numbers of children"; potential penalty up to **£18M or 10% of worldwide revenue**. For Ditto: age assurance is a Ditto pillar (gambling/gaming plus the broadening social-media age-gate surface), and this is the clearest *UK* enforcement signal yet that behavioural-guess age assurance is being ruled out — landing ~48h after the EU Special Panel's ZKP recommendation (07-15) and von der Leyen's age-rules push (07-14). The combined UK+EU direction of travel: age assurance must be *highly effective* (satisfies the regulator) **and** *privacy-preserving* (no needless PII, no face-scan for a yes/no question) — precisely the selective-disclosure / verifiable-attribute problem Ditto solves.
