### Pillar 6: Identity verification (IDV)

- **IRS proposal would let ID.me retain taxpayers' biometric scans for years — Biometric Update / Federal Register-tracked proposal, 25 May 2026** — A new IRS / ID.me Privacy Impact Assessment proposal would authorise ID.me to retain biometric scans collected during taxpayer identity verification for as long as the account is active, **plus up to 36 months after account deletion**, plus indefinitely for data tied to "high-risk transactions" in government and healthcare settings, for fraud-prevention and government-audit purposes. Government access only via law-enforcement and IG investigation through legal process. This is a substantive reversal of the 2022 IRS position (which forced ID.me to delete biometric data shortly after verification, after a Congressional firestorm and the IRS's eventual offer of a non-biometric alternative). The justification reads as a direct response to AI-generated synthetic-identity and stolen-PII attacks at federal scale: "identity fraud is becoming harder to detect as criminals use stolen personal information, synthetic, and AI-generated images to impersonate taxpayers." Significance: out-of-region for Ditto's primary EU lane, but a powerful *privacy-by-design* cross-pillar anchor. The US federal-identity stack is moving in the opposite direction to the EUDI Wallet's selective-disclosure / ZKP architecture — retain more, longer, for fraud, vs. EUDI's "prove the fact, never store the raw data" stance. The 05-25 Microsoft Research Vega ZKP framework (override in yesterday's 05-25 delta) is the *architectural* answer to exactly this problem: derive an over-18 boolean or a citizenship attribute without retaining the underlying biometric or birthdate at all.
