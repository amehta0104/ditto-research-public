## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **NIST's identity assurance levels just became auditable: ATARC published a federal transition playbook (July 21) for moving to SP 800-63 Revision 4 — days after Kantara released the conformance criteria (July 17) that let assessors certify products against Rev 4's authentication and base requirements. Rev 4's headline demands are exactly the ones an identity vendor lives or dies on: mandatory biometric injection-attack detection, phishing-resistant authenticators at AAL2, and native support for passkeys and wallets.** Account: / company. Angle: the interesting shift is that "assurance level" is moving from a self-asserted marketing claim to an **independently assessable conformance target** — a relying party can now ask "certified against 800-63B-4 at AAL2, with injection-attack detection?" and expect a real answer. POV hook: "For years 'high assurance' was whatever a vendor said it was. That era is closing. NIST's Revision 4 doesn't just recommend liveness and phishing-resistance — it makes biometric *injection-attack detection* and phishing-resistant AAL2 authenticators table stakes, and there are now published criteria to certify against them. The tell for buyers: stop accepting 'we do liveness' as an answer. Ask what the credential is certified to, at what assurance level, and whether injection attacks — the deepfake-into-the-camera-pipeline problem — are in scope of that certification. Assurance is becoming an audit, not an adjective." Note: this is US-federal-anchored (ATARC/NIST) but the assurance-level-as-conformance-target thesis is jurisdiction-agnostic and maps onto the EUDI/eIDAS2 certification track (ENISA) too. Ecosystem/standards datapoint — do not name a vendor.

### Pillar 6: Identity verification (IDV)
- **ATARC publishes a 41-page NIST SP 800-63 Revision 4 transition guide; Kantara opens Rev-4 authentication conformance — assurance levels move from guidance to auditable criteria (ATARC 21 Jul; Kantara 17 Jul, 2026)** — ATARC's Identity Management Working Group published a 41-page implementation guide (21 Jul) for federal identity/access-management teams moving from SP 800-63-3 to Revision 4, structured around NIST's five-step Digital Identity Risk Management process (define service → assess harms → select assurance levels → tailor controls → continuously evaluate) and emphasising **phishing-resistant authenticators at AAL2**. Days earlier (17 Jul), **Kantara** published the Service Assessment Criteria (SAC) and Statements of Criteria Applicability (SoCA) for **SP 800-63B-4 (Authentication and Authenticator Management)** and the **SP 800-63-4 Base Volume**, following its **800-63A-4 (Identity Proofing and Enrolment)** criteria in March — so products can now be independently assessed against Rev 4's identity-proofing *and* authentication requirements (federation, 800-63C-4, still in development). Why it matters for Ditto: Rev 4 introduces **mandatory biometric injection-attack detection (IAD)** — the deepfake/injection-into-the-camera-pipeline threat — as a requirement, plus explicit accommodation of passkeys and digital wallets. "High assurance" is becoming a certifiable target, not a claim. Directly Ditto Verify / Authenticate / orchestration-adjacent; strong fit. Standards datapoint — do not name a vendor.
