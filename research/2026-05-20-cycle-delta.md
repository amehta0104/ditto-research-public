## New findings

### Pillar 1: Banking & Payments

- **NatWest switches biometric data legal basis from consent to legitimate interests — effective 20 May 2026** — NatWest, one of the UK's four largest retail banks (~19 million customers), has changed the legal basis under which it processes biometric data (facial recognition for mobile banking; Voice ID for telephone banking) from explicit customer consent to legitimate interests under UK data protection law, effective today. Rationale: consent can be withdrawn at any time, creating operational fragility in fraud-protection flows; legitimate interests provides continuity so the bank can apply biometric authentication consistently without requiring fresh opt-ins. Customers retain the right to object. The change requires no customer action and does not alter the UX. Significance for an identity vendor: this is the first public legal-basis shift of this kind by a UK Tier-1 bank, and it normalises biometric authentication data as a standard fraud-prevention operational necessity — not a discretionary feature. It is the operational precedent that sits upstream of how regulators will treat biometric-liveness, voice-auth, and related signals in PSD3 and UK Consumer Duty enforcement.

### Pillar 7: Identity Ecosystem

- **UK Digital Access to Services Bill — King's Speech confirmation of national voluntary digital ID scheme — 13 May 2026 (catch-up; not captured in 05-13 through 05-19 deltas)** — The 2026 King's Speech included the Digital Access to Services Bill, formally committing the UK government to a voluntary national digital identity credential. Key provisions: (1) free to access, device-held, credential usable across public services and the private economy; (2) voluntary for most use cases — similar architecture to a phone-stored credential usable with selective disclosure; (3) mandatory for Right to Work checks by end of Parliament (2029); (4) launched alongside a statutory consultation on the national scheme design. The announcement drove a 212% spike in "Digital ID" search traffic in the 24 hours after the speech. Policy coverage notes that the scheme will need to interoperate with existing certified private-sector digital ID frameworks (DCMS-certified under the UK DIATF trust framework). The UK Biometrics and Surveillance Camera Commissioner and Big Brother Watch raised standard civil-liberties objections (63% of UK adults distrust government data security per polling). Significance for Ditto: the EU has an implementation deadline; the UK now has a bill. Both are legislating toward the same architecture — portable, device-bound, privacy-first credential — across two distinct but interconnected markets. Banks accepting the UK national scheme will face the same relying-party integration questions they face with EUDI. Identity vendors who can bridge both frameworks have a structural advantage.
