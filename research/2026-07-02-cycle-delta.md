### Pillar 3: Fraud / Deepfakes
- **Persona H1 2026 selfie fraud report: simple presentation attacks dominate over "fraud slop" — GenAI content in 23.5% of 27M fraudulent selfies; full fraud kits cost $12 on dark web (late June 2026; not in prior deltas)** — Persona's first-half 2026 analysis of 27 million fraudulent selfie attempts finds that, contrary to the "AI deepfakes everywhere" narrative, the most pervasive attack type by volume remains simple presentation attacks (printed photos, replay videos, masks). GenAI content appeared in 23.5% of fraudulent selfies — 20% within presentation attacks, 3.5% within injection attacks. Injection attacks represented 10.3% of all fraudulent selfie attempts (~2.8M cases). A key counter-intuitive finding: attackers are 194% more likely to inject videos of real people than AI-generated synthetic faces — authentic stolen video is more convincing and readily available than AI-generated equivalents at mainstream attack volumes. High-quality AI injection attacks are real but concentrated on high-value targets; volume fraud runs on cheaper mechanics. Full fraud packages (complete ID documents plus video selfie, assembled to defeat automated verification) are openly sold on fraud marketplaces for as little as $12, enabling batch-attack economies at scale. For Ditto: device intelligence, behavioural signals, and population-level anomaly analysis matter as much as deepfake detection for stopping volume fraud — AI deepfakes are the surgical tool, not the sledgehammer, and the $12 kit is the more prevalent threat.

### Pillar 6: LATAM
- **Brazil DeCripto (IN 2,291/2025) fully effective July 1: OECD CARF-aligned crypto-asset reporting replaces 2019 regime; abroad-domiciled VASPs serving Brazil now in scope (2026-07-01)** — Brazil's Federal Revenue Service (Receita Federal) DeCripto system — established by Instrução Normativa RFB nº 2,291/2025 in November 2025 — entered full force July 1, 2026, replacing IN 1,888/2019 which had governed crypto reporting since 2019. From July 1, individuals and legal entities must file manually in the DeCripto system for crypto-asset operations. VASPs domiciled abroad that render services to the Brazilian market are also in scope — a significant extraterritorial reach expansion. The framework aligns Brazil with the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF), requiring expanded counterparty identification, traceability of self-hosted wallet transactions, and monthly reporting on all FX and international transfers involving virtual assets. Exchanges operating in Brazil have been subject to CARF-standard automated data collection since January 1, 2026; the July 1 date triggers the user-side manual reporting phase in parallel. For Ditto/LATAM: the most significant upgrade to Brazil's crypto KYC/identity-traceability layer since the original BCB/RFB framework, arriving alongside Drex's wholesale tokenization pivot and Pix MED 2.0. Identity and KYC vendors serving Brazilian crypto exchanges and fintechs face a tightened compliance baseline from today.
