## New findings

### Pillar 1: Banking & Payments
- **Binance withdraws its MiCA licence application in Greece (Hellenic Capital Market Commission), will reapply in another EU member state — June 24, 2026, in-window; resolves the pending "Binance/HCMC" anchor** — Binance formally withdrew its Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) licence application filed with the **Hellenic Capital Market Commission (HCMC)** in Greece on **24 June 2026**, stating it will "pursue authorization in another EU Member State." The withdrawal comes exactly **seven days before the July 1, 2026 MiCA hard deadline**, by which any crypto-asset service provider must hold a licence from at least one EU member state to passport services across the 27-nation bloc — unlicensed firms "must wind down their EU activities." The move follows a June 16 Reuters report that Greek regulators planned to reject the bid over concerns about Binance's past legal issues and corporate structure; Binance had countered on June 16 that the HCMC "completed its review of the application and considered it compliant with MiCA requirements." With no formal HCMC response issued ahead of the deadline, Binance pulled the application: "We made this decision after careful consideration of the current status and timeline of the Greek process." Binance has **not disclosed which EU jurisdiction it will target next** ("We are confident we will secure a license in the coming months") and is contacting all EU users via email and in-app notifications about required actions, options, and timelines; it stressed "your funds remain safe and secure." Secondary coverage (Decrypt) frames the immediate effect as leaving EU users "in limbo" with no licensed home entity as the deadline lands. **Correction note for the record:** "HCMC" in prior deltas (June 22–24) refers to the **Hellenic Capital Market Commission (Greece)**, not Ho Chi Minh City — the pending "Binance HCMC board vote / outcome before June 30" anchor is now **resolved as a withdrawal**, not a grant or a formal rejection. For Ditto: this is the cleanest live illustration of the thesis that EU market access under MiCA turns on demonstrable compliance posture — KYC/AML, sanctions screening, corporate-structure transparency — not capital. The binding constraint for a global platform is whether it can satisfy a supervisor's identity-and-compliance bar inside a fixed window; identity orchestration + verification is the infrastructure that makes that provable. Expect a wave of named cease-operations / relocation announcements through June 30.
