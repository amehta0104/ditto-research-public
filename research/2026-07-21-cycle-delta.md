- **IDnow/Sapio Research: 51% of French and German consumers have never heard of the EUDI Wallet — but 63% say they would be more likely to use it if they knew it could prove something (e.g. age) without sharing any personal details (survey published July 16, 2026 — see integrity note)** — A survey of 2,000 respondents across France and Germany (commissioned by IDnow, conducted by Sapio Research) found: 51% have never heard of the EUDI Wallet; 27% have heard of it but are unsure what it does; only 20% have any clear understanding — this six months before the December 24, 2026 hard deadline. Concerns about data protection, security and misuse remain high even among those who do know what the wallet is; only 7% say they have no concerns whatsoever. The headline inflection point: when respondents were told the wallet would allow them to prove something — like being over 18 — *without* sharing their name, address, date of birth or any other personal detail, 63% said they would be more likely to use it. For Ditto: this is the sharpest consumer-level data point yet that the EUDI Wallet's adoption problem is not primarily a technical or regulatory problem — it is a trust-and-awareness problem — and that the feature most likely to shift consumer sentiment is exactly the selective-disclosure / privacy-preserving attribute proof that the EU's own technical architecture (ARF v2.8, OID4VP, SD-JWT) mandates. The 63% uplift on selective-disclosure framing is a usable stat in any post about why the wallet adoption gap is a UX-and-trust design challenge, not just a compliance deadline. Cross-ref the EU–Japan interoperability pilot (07-20 delta): both datapoints point to the same unsolved layer — not the protocol, but the trust and recognition experience.

### Pillar 6: LATAM
- **Brazil's national digital driver's license (CNH digital / Carteira Digital de Trânsito) gains real-time biometric verification: Hopae integrates passive facial liveness + comparison against the SENATRAN national driver registry and Brazil's federal tax authority records (July 20, 2026)** — Digital identity provider Hopae added real-time verification of Brazil's CNH digital credential to its Connect platform on July 20. The integration works as follows: a user presents the digital driver's license QR code; Hopae decodes the credential, takes a live selfie, and cross-references the face against the photograph held in Brazil's national driver's-license registry (SENATRAN) and the federal revenue authority (Receita Federal) records. The service produces a similarity score and confidence rating confirming the presenter as the legitimate credential holder, and by requiring liveness before the credential can be downloaded or modified on a device, it eliminates unauthorized third-party credential portability. For Ditto /: Brazil's national digital-identity stack — Gov.br (167M citizens enrolled), Pix (76% population, 78B transactions in 2025), CIN biometric RG replacement (mandatory rollout underway), and now CNH digital with real-time SENATRAN biometric verification — is the most complete nationally orchestrated digital-identity-and-payment infrastructure in LATAM. The CDT biometric integration is a further step in Brazil's strategy to make physical credentials redundant and to close the gap between credential issuance and holder-binding. For banks and fintechs onboarding Brazilian customers, this creates both a new high-assurance verification signal (SENATRAN cross-check available) and a new fraud vector to manage (spoofed QR code + deepfake selfie injection targeting the comparison step). Treat as a LATAM digital identity progress signal, not a vendor endorsement.
