## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **A regulator-adjacent legislature spent this week arguing that PINs and one-time codes are the humane fallback when biometrics fail. This week, malware that steals PINs, patterns and passwords from the lock screen across 140+ banking apps went from 16 targeted institutions to 349.** Zimperium zLabs published **ToxicPanda 2.0** on 2026-08-19: **349** banking, e-wallet and cryptocurrency apps targeted (up from 16 in v1.0), **16 countries**, **167 remote commands**, automated **wireless-debugging (ADB) abuse** that escalates to shell-level access through a five-phase sequence, Accessibility Service abuse for full UI theft, and a **lock-screen overlay mimicking the native Android lock screen** that captures PINs, patterns and passwords for **140+ banking and cryptocurrency applications**. Three days earlier, the Mexico City Congress formally asked CNBV and Banxico to mandate alternative authentication for elderly customers whose fingerprints no longer read — and the alternatives its legislators named were **official documents, passwords, PINs and one-time codes** (captured in the 08-19 delta; do not re-report as new). Account: (primary) / (the LATAM half — ToxicPanda's lineage is Europe-and-LATAM and the CDMX pressure is Mexican). Angle: this is **not** a "biometrics win, PINs lose" post — that framing is wrong and it contradicts the accessibility argument, which is legitimate. The POV is harder and more useful: **the industry's default answer to "biometrics excluded someone" is to fall back to a knowledge factor, and the knowledge factor is the single most actively harvested credential class on mobile right now.** An accessibility remedy that routes excluded customers onto the weakest available factor has not solved exclusion; it has concentrated the excluded population onto the attack surface with the best tooling. The structural answer is the one Ditto sells — **a fallback that is a different assurance route, not a lower assurance level**: device-bound cryptographic credentials, split-key authorisation, and a second verification path that does not degrade to a shared secret. Sub-themes: `passwordless.md` (the recovery flow is where passwordless stories fall apart), `mobile-trust.md` A/B/C, `identity-orchestration.md` A. POV hook: "This week a legislature asked banks to let people who fail a fingerprint check use a PIN instead. The same week, researchers published a banking trojan that fakes your lock screen to steal PINs across three hundred and forty-nine apps. Both stories are correct. Together they describe the trap the whole industry is in: when biometrics exclude someone, we hand them the weakest factor we have and call it inclusion. Accessibility and assurance are not opposites — but a fallback that lowers the assurance level isn't a fallback. It's a redirection to a softer target." Constraints for the drafter: **never suggest the CDMX legislators are wrong to demand a fallback** — the exclusion problem is real and Ditto's own positioning depends on it; do not name banks or claim any bank was breached by ToxicPanda (no victim institutions are named in the research); attribute the 349/16-countries/167-commands figures to **Zimperium zLabs, 2026-08-19**; and do not attach ToxicPanda 1.0's country percentages to 2.0.
