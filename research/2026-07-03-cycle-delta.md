## New findings

### Pillar 1: Banking & Payments
- **Australia AML/CTF Amendment Act Tranche 2 effective July 1, 2026: ~100,000 professional-service firms enter AUSTRAC scope, requiring digital identity verification programs for the first time** — Australia's AML/CTF Amendment Act (AUSTRAC Tranche 2) came into force July 1, 2026, extending anti-money laundering obligations beyond the financial sector to lawyers, accountants, real estate agents, trust and company service providers, and dealers in precious metals and stones for the first time in Australian history. Approximately 100,000 entities must now enroll with AUSTRAC (enrolment window: March 31 – July 29, 2026) and implement AML/CTF programs that include customer due diligence and identity verification at onboarding, ongoing transaction monitoring, and suspicious matter reporting. The reforms adopt a risk-based, outcomes-oriented model rather than prescriptive rules, and explicitly contemplate biometric verification for higher-risk clients. Record-keeping requirements no longer mandate physical ID document copies from July 1, shifting toward digital-first verification records. For Ditto: this is a direct market-creation event — professional services firms across Australia now face mandatory demand for identity verification infrastructure for the first time. The APAC IDV market gains a new mandatory compliance layer affecting four major professional sectors simultaneously. Australia is the first major English-speaking common-law jurisdiction to extend AML/KYC obligations to tranche-2 professional services at this scale.

### Pillar 2: EUDI / eIDAS2
- **Ireland closes EUDI wallet consultation and moves to pilot stage — December 24, 2026 hard deadline on track** — Ireland's Department of Public Expenditure closed its public consultation on the government's EUDI-compliant digital wallet on June 24, 2026, and has formally entered the pilot stage. The consultation ran April 3 – June 24, and Ireland is now positioned among the most advanced EU member states on readiness to meet the eIDAS 2 hard deadline of December 24, 2026. Ireland is building directly on its existing MyGovID infrastructure and plans to integrate the wallet with official national credentials. This follows a Biometric Update assessment from April 2026 rating Ireland as "on track" — the consultation close and pilot start confirms that trajectory. For Ditto: Ireland's pilot stage means relying-party integration timelines are crystallising; any vendor seeking to serve Irish financial-sector relying parties needs to track the pilot rollout and ARF conformance testing schedule.
