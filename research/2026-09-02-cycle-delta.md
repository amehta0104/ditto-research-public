## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **The US federal government just finished the argument about whether citizens should have one login, and the answer is binding: consolidate onto one platform in two years or explain yourself to the White House.** Account: / company. Angle: this **resolves the 08-28 watch item** — the OMB draft memo circulated 08-27 was finalised on **2026-08-31 as Memorandum M-26-18**. The substance is not that Login.gov won; it is **what the final text changed from the draft**. The draft made Login.gov *available alongside* existing systems. The final creates an **explicit presumption in favour of consolidation**, directs agencies to **phase out identity solutions that no longer meet operational requirements**, and requires agencies to **regularly reconsider whether alternative identity providers remain necessary**. Sharpest available line: **the draft asked agencies to add an option; the final asks them to justify keeping the ones they have.** That is the buy-vs-build and best-of-breed-vs-platform argument settled by fiat in the largest identity estate in the world — and it lands 48 hours after the UK made its own register de-listable. **Two governments in one week moved identity from "choose your provider" to "justify your provider."**

2. **The body that assigns legal identity to companies has proposed that AI agents inherit their authority from a human inside a verified organisation — not from an identity of their own.** Account:. Angle: GLEIF's **verifiable LEI** model, published 2026-09-01 alongside CEO **Alexandre Kech**'s discussion paper *Agentic AI in Payments: Establishing Interoperable Trust and Control*, argues explicitly **against** giving each agent its own distinct identity, and for a **partitioned authority** model — which agent, under whose mandate, for how long, within what limits. This is the sharpest counter yet to the "every agent needs a wallet" reflex running through the agentic-identity discourse. It also **directly extends the NIST NCCoE thread from 08-31** ("agentic AI needs a strong identity foundation, and the standards already exist"). Kech's line is the whole POV in one sentence: **"when those can be verified computationally, accountability stops depending on trust and starts depending on evidence."** For Ditto the reusable point is that **an agent is not a subject to be enrolled; it is a delegation to be bounded** — and delegation with a duration and a limit is an orchestration problem, not a credential-issuance problem.

3. **A telecoms infrastructure vendor and a payments processor just bundled onboarding, identity proofing, KYC, authentication, card issuing and the core ledger into a single turnkey product — and it already runs 131 million users.** Account: / company. ### Pillar 2: Identity orchestration

- **GLEIF published a model in which an AI agent proves which organisation stands behind it, what authority was delegated, and for how long — explicitly rejecting the idea that each agent needs its own identity (2026-09-01).** The **Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation** published a working paper, **"Agentic AI in Payments: Establishing Interoperable Trust and Control,"** and an accompanying blog post, **"When AI agents transact, who stands behind them? Why verifiable organizational identity is critical to agentic payments,"** both dated 2026-09-01, authored by CEO **Alexandre Kech**. Mechanics: the **verifiable LEI (vLEI)** uses a **cryptographic trust chain leading back to a verified individual within a verified organisation**, providing machine-readable proof of three things — **the legal identity of the organisation an agent acts for, the authority delegated to that agent, and the boundaries of that authority.** It operates as **a trust layer above device and agent identity** — i.e. it composes with, rather than replaces, whatever credential the agent itself holds. The governance model is **"partitioned authority": controlling which agent can do what, under whose mandate, for how long, and according to what limitations** — which Kech argues is **more efficient than arming each agent with its own distinct identity.** It generates **auditable records** and supports **"privacy-preserving yet compliant execution across multiple platforms."** Kech, on the core claim: **"when those can be verified computationally, accountability stops depending on trust and starts depending on evidence."** The stated infrastructure gap: **"which organization stands behind an agent and who authorized it to act,"** as agent-initiated transactions scale across payments, trade, supply chains, digital assets and government services. Why it matters for Ditto: this is the **third independent source in six days** converging on the same architecture — NIST NCCoE (08-31: the standards already exist, implement them), CrowdStrike/CLEAR (08-31: high-assurance verification is one signal among several, fired mid-session), and now GLEIF (09-01: authority is delegated and bounded, not issued). **The synthesis worth owning: the agentic-identity problem is being solved as a delegation and orchestration problem, not as an enrolment problem — and the vendors selling "an identity for every agent" are answering a question the standards bodies have stopped asking.** ⚠️ **Constraint: this is a discussion paper and a proposed model, not a standard, a mandate or a deployment. Write "GLEIF has proposed," never "the vLEI model requires."** GLEIF appears in the corpus only in the 2026-05-06 baseline and the 05-22 delta — **this is effectively a first capture of GLEIF on the agentic thread.**
