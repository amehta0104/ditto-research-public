Account: company /. Angle: this is what "appropriate age assurance" degrades into when the obligation lands on a distribution layer that has a payment instrument already on file and no identity stack. **A credit card is not an age check. It is a proxy for one, inherited from a bank that did the actual verification years ago and for a different purpose** — and it fails exactly the populations age assurance exists to protect, while excluding adults who don't hold one. Pairs with the UK device-level legislation (09-10): two jurisdictions pushing age assurance down the stack, and the layers receiving it solving it with whatever they already had.

3. **Microsoft is tracking active cloud breaches in which the phishing lure is the passkey enrolment itself — and is explicit that the cryptography never failed.** Published 2026-09-09: since **May 2026**, actors tracked as **Storm-3121** and **Storm-3032** call or text employees on personal phones posing as IT helpdesk, claim a passkey/MFA/SSO configuration must be updated urgently, and route them into adversary-in-the-middle pages or device-code flows. Account: / company. This is the exact argument yesterday's Singpass finding set up from the other side: **phishing resistance is a property of the whole lifecycle, not of the key.** You do not get it by shipping passkeys. You get it by making enrolment and recovery as hard to socially engineer as the authentication you just hardened.

### Pillar 9: Passwordless / split-key

- **Microsoft is tracking active cloud intrusions in which the phishing pretext is the passkey enrolment itself — and states plainly that the cryptography was never defeated.** Published on the **Microsoft Security Blog, 2026-09-09**; trade coverage 09-10. Since **May 2026**, actors tracked as **Storm-3121** (linked to theft leading to extortion by **ShinyHunters** and **Falcon**) and **Storm-3032** (linked to the **Helix** extortion operation) have placed calls or SMS to employees' **personal phone numbers**, posing as the organisation's IT helpdesk and creating urgency around a **passkey, MFA or SSO configuration that "must be updated immediately to avoid disruption."** Victims are routed to a site closely resembling the legitimate Microsoft sign-in experience; credentials are captured via **adversary-in-the-middle** pages or the victim is induced to authorise an **attacker-controlled device via device-code flow**. Post-compromise, the actors **register additional authentication methods and enrol devices for persistence**, then collect at volume from **Exchange, SharePoint and OneDrive** through **Microsoft Graph**, exfiltrating over hours or days at controlled rates to evade detection. **Microsoft's own qualification is the most important sentence in the report: the campaign "generally does not break passkey cryptography or interfere with a genuine passkey enrollment."** **This is the finding, and it must be written honestly or not at all.** Passkeys did not fail. **The enrolment and recovery ceremony around them failed, and it failed because it is still a human conversation.** For a vendor selling phishing-resistant authentication this is uncomfortable and therefore credible: **the phishing resistance of a credential is capped by the weakest path that can create or replace one.** It pairs exactly with the 09-10 Singpass finding from the opposite direction — there the fallback was a retained SMS OTP; here the fallback is a helpdesk call. **Same structural hole, two different jurisdictions, one week.**

### Pillar 11: Age assurance & privacy attributes

- **Australia's app-store age-assurance obligations became enforceable on 9 September 2026, and the first major implementation to appear verifies adulthood by requiring a credit card on file — with no ID or facial-estimation alternative offered.** The **Age-Restricted Material App Distribution Services Code** has been in force since **2026-03-09**, but its **age assurance obligations commenced on 2026-09-09** after a six-month transition: app distribution services must apply appropriate age assurance **before** a user can download or purchase an 18+ rated app. Enforcement sits with the **eSafety Commissioner**; ignoring a formal direction can trigger a civil penalty of up to **A$49.5 million**. Reported **2026-09-10**: **Steam** now requires Australian users to **add and retain a valid Australian credit card on the account** to view or purchase R18+ titles, with the check applied **at the account and storefront layer before the restricted store page is shown**. The reporting is explicit that **"the workflow does not present government-ID verification or facial age estimation as alternative methods."** Valve previously used the same mechanism for UK users. A competing games storefront has taken the opposite route, offering estimation and document-verification options through certified providers. **The finding is what "appropriate age assurance" becomes when the obligation lands on a layer that has no identity stack and does have a payment instrument.** A credit card is not an age check — **it is a second-hand inference from a bank that verified someone years ago for an entirely different purpose**, and it has two failure modes that matter: it admits any adult's card in a child's hands, and it excludes adults who don't hold credit. **Read it next to the UK device-level legislation announced 09-09 and the pattern is sharp: two governments have pushed age assurance down the stack to platforms and stores, and the receiving layers are solving it with whatever they already had on file.** The regulatory instrument moved; the assurance did not necessarily follow.
