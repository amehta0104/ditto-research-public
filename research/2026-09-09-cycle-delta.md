## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **The operating system has started answering "how old is this user?" — and Microsoft is now also offering to answer "and is that verified?"** Microsoft launched the Windows Age API on 2026-09-08. Apps get one of five age bands and explicitly never a date of birth; a second method returns *verified* age status; a Microsoft-operated verification service sits behind it for high-confidence adult status. Account: / company. Angle: for a decade the industry has argued that selective disclosure is the right architecture and been told it was academic. Three operating systems now ship it as a platform primitive. The question for a regulated buyer is no longer whether attribute-only disclosure is viable — it is why their own identity stack still asks for a full date of birth.

2. **An identity verification provider has confirmed that the contents of its cloud — 170 million scanned identity documents, including infrared and ultraviolet captures — were taken, and the marketplace selling them was still being restocked in near-real time.** IDScan.net acknowledged the breach on 2026-09-08; the dataset is 153m driver's licences, 10m ID cards, 3m travel documents and 579,000 medical cards. Account: company /. Angle: every one of those documents was scanned to answer a question that had already been answered. The breach is not a failure of verification — the verification worked. It is a failure of *retention*. You cannot lose what you never stored.

3. **Parliament's own research service has now put in writing what the UK identity industry has been arguing since the GOV.UK Wallet guidance landed: derived credentials have no digital signature, and that makes them fundamentally less valuable than the real thing.** The House of Commons Library's updated "Digital ID in the UK" briefing (2026-09-08) sets the DVS sector at £2bn revenue in 2024/25 and states the signature gap plainly. Account: / company. Angle: a £2bn certified sector has just been told by the legislature's neutral research arm that the credential it is permitted to issue is a photocopy. Ask your identity vendor which one they hand you — and whether anyone downstream can verify it cryptographically.

### Pillar 11: Age assurance & privacy attributes

- **Microsoft launched the Windows Age API on 2026-09-08 — apps receive one of five age bands and never a date of birth, a second method returns verified age status, and Microsoft is operating its own verification service behind it.** The bands are **under 10, 10–12, 13–15, 16–17 and 18+**. Microsoft's stated design is that the APIs are "designed for apps that need to comply with child safety regulations without requiring the app to collect, store, or process date of birth or other personally identifiable information", and that "apps receive only the age-related signal needed for the experience and not sensitive personal data such as full date of birth." Three capabilities are named: **`GetUserAgeRangeAsync`** (non-PII age group), **`GetAgeVerificationStatusAsync`** (verified age status), and **`CheckAgeStatusAsync`**, which "will be available with a future update." Microsoft distinguishes declared from verified age — "in many cases, understanding age is not enough" — and points to **Microsoft Age Verification (MAV)** for "high confidence that a user is an adult." Documentation is at `aka.ms/windows-age-api`. **This resolves the corpus's top open watch item, and it makes Windows the third OS to ship attribute-only age disclosure after Apple and Google.** The genuinely new element beyond the 09-07 documentation finding is the *verified* status method and the platform-operated verification service behind it — the OS is no longer only relaying a declared bracket, it is offering to be the assurance provider.

  - Source: https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/microsoft-launches-age-api-following-os-level-declared-age-range-model
  - Date: 2026-09-08
  - **Accuracy rule: Insiders-broad now, general availability "soon", `CheckAgeStatusAsync` not yet shipped. Do not write "generally available."**

- **A global games storefront turned on four parallel age-assurance routes for R18+ titles in Australia today, and built it so the platform itself never receives the selfie or the document.** The obligation flows from Australia's **Age-Restricted Material Codes, in force since 2026-03-09**, which require online gaming platforms to run age assurance for R18+ classified games; offline single-player titles are out of scope, and penalties reach **A$49.5m**. The four routes are facial age estimation from a selfie, a payment-card check via a **5-cent authorisation**, a government ID document check, and a **bank-backed age confirmation via ConnectID**, the last scheduled for later in September. Existing purchases are unaffected. The architectural detail that matters: the storefront's own kids-services layer "does not receive the selfie" or the identity documents, and images are "encrypted and deleted immediately" after verification. **This is the most complete public worked example of multi-route age assurance under a national mandate**, and it demonstrates something Ditto's buyers keep asking about — that offering a genuine non-biometric alternative (card, bank) alongside facial estimation is operationally achievable, which is precisely what the Spanish DPA demanded and a certified provider said was impossible (09-04 delta, Pillar 6/11).
  - Source: https://idtechwire.com/epic-adds-yoti-and-veriff-age-checks-for-australian-r18-games/
  - Date: 2026-09-08 (announced), 2026-09-09 (effective)

- **The IEEE is drafting the first cross-jurisdictional standard for online parental consent, with four graded confidence levels and a certification path — chaired by the head of the age-verification industry's trade body.** **IEEE P2089.3** is an active project covering system design, specification, evaluation and deployment for managing and verifying parental consent. It defines four confidence levels — **asserted, standard, enhanced and strict** — plus conformance testing and certification under **ISO/IEC 17065**, and privacy/data-security requirements specific to consent. It is standardising two dimensions: who gives consent and to what, meaning assurance levels for the consent-giver-to-minor relationship *and* a taxonomy of the content or functionality being approved. The working group is chaired by **Iain Corby**, executive director of the **Age Verification Providers Association**, and explicitly "began with a blank sheet of paper, not constrained by any specific law" — the stated aim being to modernise past a 30-year-old COPPA framing. No publication date. **Parental consent is the part of age assurance nobody has standardised, and it is the harder problem: proving an adult is an adult is a single-subject check, while proving one person is entitled to consent on behalf of another is a relationship attestation** — which is a verifiable-credential problem, not a liveness problem.
  - Source: https://www.biometricupdate.com/202609/ieee-developing-parental-consent-standard-for-online-age-assurance
  - Date: 2026-09-08
  - **Note: the AVPA is an interested party and its executive director chairs the group. Attribute, do not assert neutrality.**
