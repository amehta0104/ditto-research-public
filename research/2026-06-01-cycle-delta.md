### Pillar 10: ZKPs in practice

- **OpenID Connect Advanced Syntax for Claims (ASC) 1.0 formally approved as an OpenID Implementer's Draft — 61 approve / 0 object / 30 abstain (91 of 425 members, 21.4%, clearing the 20% quorum); OpenID Foundation result page published 27 May 2026** — the multi-cycle carry-forward (member vote concluded 15 May; flagged "formal result still not posted" in the 05-27/05-28/05-29 deltas) has now **resolved**: the OpenID Foundation's "Implementer's Draft Approved" page is live. ASC 1.0 is the product of the eKYC & IDA Working Group and has two independently-implementable components: **"Selective Abort and Omit"** and **"Transformed Claims."** Transformed Claims is the on-pillar piece — it lets a relying party request a *derived* attribute (e.g. an over-18 boolean computed from a birthdate) rather than the raw underlying claim, i.e. selective-disclosure / data-minimisation at the protocol layer. Significance: this is exactly the "ZKP-in-practice, not academic theory" artifact the pillar exists to surface — a stable, citable, primary-source standard that operationalises privacy-preserving claim derivation, and the protocol-layer complement to the cryptographic-primitive story (05-26 Microsoft Research Vega ZKP framework, 212ms proving / 51ms verification). The drafter can now reference ASC 1.0 as a confirmed milestone rather than a pending vote. **Note: ASC 1.0 is an Implementer's Draft (a stable IP-protected interop checkpoint), not yet a Final Specification — frame it as "approved Implementer's Draft," not "final standard."**
