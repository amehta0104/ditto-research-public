## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **The EU just grew a layer nobody has been building for: proving *facts about* a customer, not just *who* they are — and the first commercial issuer cleared its audit with zero non-conformities, 22 months before AMLR makes it mandatory.** On **2026-08-21** (trade pickup 08-24) a European identity provider's Swedish trust-services subsidiary completed conformity assessment to issue **Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes (QEAAs)** — machine-readable, legally recognised credentials attesting specific verified facts such as **residential address, tax identification number, or professional qualification**. Assessment ran against **ETSI EN 319 401** and **ETSI TS 119 471**, covering attribute proofing, technical issuance, lifecycle management and IT security, with onsite visits and **zero non-conformities**; the conformity report is now with **Sweden's Post- och telestyrelsen (PTS)** for qualified status and EU Trust List entry. The attestations can be delivered into existing onboarding workflows **or issued directly to EUDI Wallets**. The stated commercial driver is **AMLR, applicable 2027-07-10**, which requires regulated firms to collect and verify customer attributes that standard eID schemes were never built to carry. Account: (primary) / company. Angle: this is the **cleanest available proof that identity and attributes are separating into two different products**, and that the second one is where the 2027 compliance pressure actually lands. Every eID scheme in Europe answers "is this person who they claim to be." **AMLR Article 28 asks a different question: prove this specific fact about them, to a standard, across 27 member states.** A passport photo page does not answer that. A national eID does not answer that. The QEAA layer is the EU's answer, and the fact that a commercial issuer just cleared a full ETSI audit means the layer stops being architecture and starts being procurement. POV hook: "Knowing who your customer is and proving a fact about your customer are not the same problem, and Europe has just written them into two different regulations with two different deadlines. Wallets went live under eIDAS 2. Attributes come due under AMLR in July 2027. Most institutions are still solving the first one." Second hook: **the attestation is reusable and machine-readable** — which is `customer-onboarding.md` Sub-theme A ("verify once, reuse trust") arriving as a legal instrument rather than a vendor feature. Constraints: **the provider is a direct competitor under `competitors.md` and must never be named** — frame at category level ("the first qualified attribute attestations are clearing audit"). **Do not say it is on the EU Trust List or holds qualified status** — the conformity report has only been submitted; PTS has not granted. **Date it 2026-08-21.** Also note this cuts *toward* Ditto's ZKP thesis and *against* it simultaneously: QEAAs make attribute-level proof legally portable, which is the argument Ditto makes — but a qualified attestation still discloses the attribute value to the relying party, where selective disclosure and ZKP-backed predicates would not. **That tension is the most interesting thing in this finding** and the honest version of the post says so.
