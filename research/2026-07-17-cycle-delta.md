### Pillar 11: Age assurance & privacy attributes
- **US "Facial Recognition to Protect Children Act" would mandate facial *age estimation* for online sportsbooks and prediction markets — a transatlantic contrast to the EU/UK move away from face-based checks (introduced July 15, 2026)** — Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ) introduced the bipartisan Facial Recognition to Protect Children Act, unveiled alongside Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour and a bipartisan group of co-sponsors. It would require online sportsbooks and prediction markets to scan a user's facial structure to *estimate* whether they are old enough to bet — the bill's office stresses the system would not store identity or biometric data, only an age estimate (technically facial age *estimation*, not recognition). Rationale: rising underage gambling (Common Sense Media — 36% of boys 11–17 gambled in the past year, 40% for 14–17). For Ditto: age assurance is a Ditto pillar and gambling/gaming is a core ICP, so this is directly relevant — but the more interesting angle is the **transatlantic divergence within one week**: US legislators are reaching for facial age *estimation* for gambling at the exact moment the EU Special Panel recommended *against* biometric age-estimation in favour of zero-knowledge proofs (07-15) and Ofcom ruled behavioural *inference* not "highly effective" (07-16). POV hook: "Same problem — keep minors off gambling and adult platforms — three regulators, three different answers in one week. The EU's child-safety panel said don't scan the face, use a zero-knowledge proof. Ofcom said don't guess from behaviour, use a highly-effective method. A bipartisan US bill said scan the face but don't store it. The one thing they agree on is the destination: prove the age claim, minimise the data. The disagreement is purely about *method* — and the method that satisfies the most regimes at once is the one that answers the yes/no age question without collecting or estimating a face at all." Proposed legislation (not enacted) — directional policy datapoint; do not name a vendor.
