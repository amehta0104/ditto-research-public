### Pillar 6: Identity verification (IDV)

- **The UK's Digital Verification Services Trust Framework v1.0 comes into force today, creating the first statutory certification regime — and the first de-listable register — for identity providers in the UK (2026-09-01).** Published by the **Office for Digital Identities and Attributes (OfDIA)** within **DSIT**, v1.0 is **the first revised statutory trust framework published under powers in the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025**; the final text was published on GOV.UK in **June 2026**. Commencement: **"1 September 2026, or from the date at least one conformity assessment body (CAB) is accredited against the 1.0 certification scheme, whichever is later"** ⚠️ (see provenance note — the CAB gate is unverified). Mechanics, all from the gov.uk primary: **until 1 September new services were evaluated against v0.4; after that date all new services certify against 1.0 only.** Existing v0.4 services **must** uplift and **every service has at least 15 months** to do so; at their next scheduled evaluation they may **either retain 0.4 for an additional year or uplift**, and **at the following evaluation uplift is mandatory** — **failure results in loss of certified status and removal from the Digital Verification Services register.** A **delta assessment** allows faster uplift where the service completed a full 0.4 audit last year and runs a three-year evaluation cycle. **Providers certified under 1.0 gain entitlement to use the DVS trust mark for the first time.** Scope covers **AML compliance, right-to-work, right-to-rent and DBS background screening** — and, newly, **businesses must use age verification from certified DVS providers when requiring proof of age for alcohol purchases under new licensing conditions.** Sector size, as reported: **275 UK digital identity firms generating approximately £2.027 billion in annual revenue** (OfDIA sector research via trade press — attribute, do not present as an independent figure). One certified provider's assessment of the real obstacle, worth using **unattributed**: the framework gives the market "proper legal footing," but the harder problem is **convincing regulated businesses — particularly banks running legacy systems — to outsource identity verification to specialist providers rather than keep it in-house.** Why it matters for Ditto: this is the home-market regulatory event of the year and it converts identity certification from a marketing badge into **a listed status with a removal mechanism**. **The strongest angle is not "get certified" — it is that the buy-vs-build question just acquired a regulatory answer.** A bank running identity checks in-house is not on the register at all, and from today the register is where alcohol licensing, right-to-work and AML checks are told to look.

- **California's legislature voted unanimously to exempt open-source operating systems from its device-level age-signal duty, narrowing the first law that makes the OS the age oracle (2026-08-31).** **AB 1856**, authored by Assembly Member **Buffy Wicks**, amends the **Digital Age Assurance Act (AB 1043)**, which **takes effect 2027-01-01**. The underlying duty: **operating system providers must present an interface at account setup requesting the user's birth date or age, then broadcast a signal indicating the age bracket — under 13, 13–15, 16–17, or 18+ — to app stores and application developers via secure API or the operating system itself.** The Act also **prohibits entities from requesting an age signal from an OS provider or app store unless required by law.** AB 1856 exempts operating systems **"under license terms that permit a recipient to copy, redistribute, and modify the software"** — covering GPL, MIT, BSD and Apache-licensed distributions such as Debian, Fedora, Ubuntu, Arch and the BSD family, and **explicitly not Windows, macOS, iOS or Android.** Passage: **Senate 39-0, Assembly 69-0**, no dissenting votes; **awaiting the Governor's signature** after engrossing and enrolling. The **Electronic Frontier Foundation** and open-source developers opposed the original compliance burden, and **the Senate stripped provisions that would have extended requirements to web browsers and website operators.** Why it matters: AB 1043 is the most consequential age-assurance architecture in the corpus because it **moves the age signal below the application layer into the OS**, which is a fundamentally different model from either document checks or facial estimation. **The interesting part of AB 1856 is what the carve-out reveals: the duty only works where there is a commercial entity to hold responsible.** A volunteer-maintained OS cannot be an age oracle, so it is exempted — which means the design depends on platform concentration, not on the technology. **The reusable POV: an age signal that only functions on operating systems with a corporate owner is a compliance mechanism, not an identity mechanism.** ⚠️ **Constraints: not yet law — write "passed both chambers and awaits signature," never "California has exempted."
