### Pillar 3: EUDI / eIDAS2
- **Mercosur approves mutual recognition of national digital IDs across Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay (July 14, 2026, Asunción summit) — a "LATAM eIDAS" cross-border interoperability accord for 270M+ residents** — Meeting at the Mercosur summit in Asunción, the bloc approved mutual recognition of high-security electronic identification and authentication methods for individuals: once ratified by each member state's national congress, a national digital ID issued in one country will carry full legal validity in the others, letting citizens and businesses complete official procedures and access digital services cross-border without travelling or presenting paper documents. The framework sets shared standards for security, electronic authentication, validation, personal-data protection and high-level assurance, and mandates interoperability between national systems coordinated through designated authorities. It caps a technical process launched under Paraguay's 2024 Pro Tempore Presidency and finalised at the June 2026 Digital Agenda Group meeting; Bolivia and Chile are already working to join the Mercosur Digital Citizen mechanism. For Ditto: this is the clearest LATAM analogue to eIDAS2's cross-border wallet-acceptance architecture — the same relying-party trust problem (how does an institution in one country accept a credential issued by another without re-collecting raw PII) that Ditto's selective-disclosure and verification stack addresses, now a live regional mandate across four of South America's largest economies. It reframes "EUDI-style interoperability" as a global pattern, not an EU peculiarity — useful's LATAM POV. Ecosystem/policy datapoint — do not name a vendor.

### Pillar 11: Age assurance & privacy attributes
- **EU Special Panel on Child Safety Online's final report: build age assurance on zero-knowledge proofs, reject document processing and biometric age-estimation, deliver via the EU Digital Identity Wallet (July 14, 2026)** — The expert panel advising the European Commission presented its final report, recommending an EU-wide floor keeping under-13s off social media and — critically for identity vendors — that the age checks enforcing it be built on zero-knowledge proofs rather than ID documents or biometric age estimation. The panel recommends *against* processing identity documents or biometric data to estimate age, favouring ZKP standards that let a user confirm they meet a minimum age without disclosing identity or personal data to platforms or third-party verification providers; the mechanism could be integrated into the EU Digital Identity Wallet and overseen by the European Board for Digital Services through common benchmarks and mandatory audits. This sharpens the 07-14 von der Leyen item (she received this panel's report July 13 and pointed to the EU's "privacy-preserving, open-source" age-verification app) into an explicit, named ZKP endorsement — and it explicitly rules out the two approaches most platforms are deploying (document upload; face-based age estimation). For Ditto: age assurance is a Ditto pillar (gambling/gaming plus the broadening age-gate surface incl. social media), and this is the strongest policy signal yet that the EU is standardising on cryptographic, selective-disclosure age proof — the same wallet-attribute/ZKP direction as the Bulgaria under-16 bill (07-10), the EU age-verification blueprint, and Gataca's ISO/IEC 27566 age-assurance certification (07-13). It broadens the addressable age-assurance mandate from gambling/adult content into mainstream social platforms and hands Ditto a policy-grounded POV for its ZKP pillar. Ecosystem/policy datapoint — do not name a vendor.
