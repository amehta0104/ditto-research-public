### Pillar 7: Identity ecosystem
- **UK Office for Digital Identities and Attributes publishes annual digital identity sectoral analysis 2026: £2B revenue, 275 firms, 81% consumer awareness, with the policy adrift after Starmer's departure** — Published July 8, 2026. Key headline data: the UK digital identity sector generated an estimated £2 billion in annual revenue in 2024–25; 275 firms are providing digital identity products and services (net +9 since baseline); Gross Value Added reached £1 billion (+17% YoY); GVA per employee rose to £107,800 (from £86,600); full-time employment fell from 10,246 to 9,624 FTEs, consistent with market consolidation and automation rather than sector contraction. Consumer adoption: 81% of respondents reported understanding of digital identity (up from 71% at baseline); 77% reported having used a digital identity service for at least one purpose. Service mix shift: professional and credential verification rose from 28% to 55% of provider offerings; age assurance from 23% to 31%. Demand concentration: 90% of providers report at least one customer in financial and professional services, confirming that identity-led financial compliance is the sector's commercial anchor. Political context: the report lands on the same day Labour leadership nominations opened (July 9), following Keir Starmer's June 22 resignation. Starmer's government was the principal political advocate for the mandatory right-to-work digital ID scheme; with that agenda now in flux, the DVS Trust Framework 1.0 (certification-only, not mandatory) enforcement date of September 1 carries forward, but the trajectory of any future mandatory scheme is uncertain pending a new PM. For Ditto: the £2B revenue figure and 77% consumer usage rate are the most quotable numbers yet for making the UK identity market concrete to an audience of financial-services decision-makers. The political dislocation is the risk narrative — DVS providers certified against a trust framework that was politically backstopped now face an enforcement deadline with no confirmed policy direction beyond it.

- **Malaysia's Zetrix AI signs MoU with the Philippines' Department of Information and Communications Technology to power the Philippines' national public blockchain — with initial use case in cross-border digital ID interoperability and government credential verification** — Announced July 7, 2026. Zetrix Philippines Inc. and My Blockchain Infrastructure Sdn. Bhd. (a Zetrix AI joint venture with Malaysia's national research agency MIMOS Berhad) signed an MoU with the Philippines DICT to establish the Philippines' national public blockchain using the Zetrix Layer-1 protocol. The Philippines becomes the second sovereign government to adopt the Zetrix protocol for national blockchain infrastructure, after Malaysia. Planned use cases: (1) cross-border digital ID interoperability between Malaysia and the Philippines — enabling citizens of both countries to present verified government credentials across borders; (2) issuance and verification of Philippines government credentials on-chain; (3) trade-facilitation applications. For Ditto: this is a government-scale instantiation of the pattern Ditto's technology is designed to complement — selective disclosure of verifiable credentials issued by a sovereign authority, anchored to a public ledger, with cross-border interoperability. The Malaysia-Philippines bilateral is a live proof point that sovereign digital identity is moving from national wallet pilots (EUDI) to inter-sovereign credential portability in APAC. The architecture raises the same questions Ditto addresses for EU: who issues the identity claim, how is it cryptographically bound, and what does the relying party need to verify without re-collecting the raw PII.
