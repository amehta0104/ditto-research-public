## Override-worthy this cycle

1. **Identity-verification vendors have started registering as regulated European trust infrastructure — two of them, in one week, through the same Swedish supervisor. The category is not adding a feature. It is changing what kind of legal entity it is.** On **2026-08-25** a European IDV provider's Swedish subsidiary was **granted qualified trust service provider status under eIDAS and entered on the EU Trusted List**, supervised by **Post- och telestyrelsen (PTS)**, enabling it to issue **Qualified Electronic Signature certificates directly** rather than routing through an external trust provider. Four days earlier, a **different** European IDV provider — a direct competitor — completed conformity assessment through **its own Swedish subsidiary, under the same authority**, to issue **Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes** (captured in the 08-25 delta; that one is submitted to PTS, not yet granted). Two instruments, two companies, one supervisor, one week. The stated commercial driver in both cases is the same: **AMLR, applicable 2027-07-10**, which recognises eIDAS-compliant electronic identification and qualified trust services as valid customer due diligence, and which asks obliged entities to prove verified facts that no national eID scheme carries. The second-order detail is the sharper one: by holding qualified status itself, the provider can complete **verification and legally binding signature inside a single continuous session with no SMS one-time passcode** — the OTP step existing only because an intermediary trust provider demanded it. Account: (primary) / company. Angle: **the IDV market is being pulled across a regulatory line.** For a decade, identity vendors were suppliers to regulated entities. AMLR and eIDAS 2 together create a category where the vendor is **itself** a supervised entity on a public EU trust list, audited against ETSI standards, with a named national supervisor. That is a different business, a different cost base, a different procurement conversation, and — critically for Ditto — **a different competitive moat than accuracy benchmarks.** A buyer choosing between two providers where one is on the EU Trusted List and one is not is no longer comparing false-accept rates. POV hook: "Two identity vendors became regulated trust providers this week, through the same Swedish supervisor, four days apart. That is not a coincidence and it is not a product launch. AMLR comes into application in July 2027 and it recognises qualified trust services as customer due diligence. The vendors that read the regulation early are not building a feature — they are becoming a supervised entity. In eighteen months, 'are you on the EU Trusted List' will be a procurement question, and the honest answer for most of the market will be no." Second hook — **the OTP is a supply-chain artefact**: the SMS one-time passcode in that signing flow existed because of an intermediary, not because of a security requirement. Remove the intermediary and the friction disappears without any loss of assurance. That is `passwordless.md`'s argument arriving from an unexpected direction, and it is a genuinely non-obvious point. Third angle, and the most Ditto-specific: **qualified status is disclosure-maximal.** A QES binds a legal identity to a document; a QEAA discloses an attribute value to the relying party. Both are legally portable and neither is minimal. `zkp-in-practice.md` argues for proving the *predicate* rather than revealing the *value*. The EU has just built a fast lane for the disclosure model, and the privacy-preserving model has no equivalent legal instrument. **That gap is the most interesting thing on this board and nobody is writing about it.** Constraints: **name neither vendor** — one is on `competitors.md`, the other is a direct EU IDV competitor merging with Veridas; frame the whole argument at category level ("two European identity vendors", "the category"). **Do not conflate QES with QEAA.** The granted status applies to the 08-25 provider only; the 08-21 provider's status is **submitted, not granted**. Do not claim Ditto holds or is pursuing qualified trust status.

The package also introduces a **third-party exclusion scheme** letting family members — and police, on money-laundering grounds — initiate an exclusion; raises the gaming machine entitlement forfeiture rate from **one in three to one in two**; ends **VIP programs** tied to gaming; bans gambling advertising on government and council assets; prohibits unsolicited direct marketing; and funds a two-year upgrade of the **Centralised Monitoring System** for account-based play. Funding: **$95.2 million over four years**, plus **$20 million over two years** to the Responsible Gambling Fund. Minister **David Harris** described them as *"common sense reforms developed alongside industry, harm minimisation groups, stakeholders and privacy experts"* operating under *"really strict guidelines"*, developed with input from the **Office of the Australian Information Commissioner** and the **NSW Privacy Commissioner**. Venues have been operating under a **non-enforceable facial-recognition code of practice** issued by **Liquor & Gaming NSW since March 2026**; this converts that voluntary code into a mandate. NSW is a regulator mandating the exact opposite, and **it is not being unreasonable**, which is what makes it interesting. A self-exclusion register cannot be satisfied by "this person is over 18." It has to answer "is this specific person on a list," which requires **identification, not attestation**, at every door, for every patron, including the overwhelming majority who are not on the register.
